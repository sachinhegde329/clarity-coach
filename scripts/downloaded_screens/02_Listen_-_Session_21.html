"Name it first."

Pre-empting objections isn't about apologizing; it's about tactical empathy. By stating the negative emotion they might be feeling out loud, you instantly defuse its power and create a safe environment for truth.

Transcript

00:00 When we step into a negotiation, our instinct is to present our case logically. We want to show the data, prove we are right, and ignore any tension.

00:18 But the human brain doesn't process logic when it feels threatened. If they expect you to be aggressive, or if they know they've missed a deadline, that fear is sitting right between you.

00:42

This is where we use the Accusation Audit. By naming the negative emotion or the "elephant in the room" first, you disarm it entirely.

01:05 You might start by saying, "You're probably going to think I'm the most demanding partner ever for bringing this up."

01:22 Suddenly, whatever you say next isn't as bad as their imagination. You've placed a boundary around the fear, and you've shown that you understand their perspective.

01:45 Don't run from the negatives. Lean into them. Audit the accusations they are harboring against you, and state them clearly. It is the fastest path to trust.
