The Aikido Pivot

Step 2: Listen. Acknowledge and redirect. Learn to absorb conversational momentum rather than blocking it.

Tidbit

Instead of saying "No, but...", try "I see your point, and...". This is the core of the Aikido pivot.

Transcript

When you're in a high-stakes meeting, interruptions are inevitable. The instinct is to either fold or fight back aggressively.

Both approaches kill your momentum. What we want to employ here is something I call the Aikido pivot.

First, you must acknowledge the interruption. Don't ignore it. Validate their input briefly, then immediately redirect to your point using a transitional conjunction.

For example, "That's a valid concern regarding the Q3 numbers, and looking at our overall strategy..."

You've absorbed their energy without letting it knock you off balance. Now, let's practice this timing.
