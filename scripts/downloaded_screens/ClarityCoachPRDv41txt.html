Clarity Coach Product Requirements Document v4 Voice-first communication coach for mid-career professionals. A six-sprint training programme of 36 daily sessions. Deterministic feedback for everyone; AI-powered personalisation for premium. Build target: 8 to 10 weeks. Solo developer with AI assistance. iOS and Android. Changes from v3.1: the curriculum is now structured as six themed sprints across 36 sessions, replacing the floor metaphor. The Journey screen replaces the building map. Vocabulary updated throughout: sessions (not days), sprints (not floors). Sprint themes are named with verbs the user can identify with becoming. 1. Executive summary Clarity Coach is a six-sprint training programme that teaches mid-career professionals to communicate clearly under pressure. The user commits to a single 5-minute ritual per session, repeated for 36 sessions, organised into six themed sprints of six sessions each. The product spine. Every session follows the same five steps in the same order: Centre, Listen, Do, See, Commit. No skips, no shortcuts. The structure is deliberately repetitive because discipline is the brand promise. What changes across the 36 sessions is the content selected for each step, sequenced by sprint theme. The six sprints. Notice, Steady, Lead, Hold, Compose, Perform. Each sprint runs six sessions: five practice sessions followed by a Review session that consolidates the sprint. The verbs name what the user can demonstrably do at the end of each sprint, not what the app does for them. Freemium architecture. All AI-generated content sits behind a paid subscription. The free tier is a complete product, powered by deterministic metric analysis on transcripts plus a brief flash-model critique per session. Premium adds deeper AI commentary, weekly personalised prompts, adversarial Hot Seat simulations, and AI-narrated forensic reports. Pricing and margins. Free; 9.99 USD per month; 59 USD per year; 149 USD lifetime. Heavy premium user costs roughly 0.20 to 0.25 USD per month in API costs. Net margin per premium user exceeds 85 per cent after platform fees. Apply for Apple Small Business Programme on day one to secure the 15 per cent rate. Build constraints. Solo developer using AI tooling, 8 to 10 week MVP. Sprints 1 to 3 (Notice, Steady, Lead) ship in v1 covering 18 sessions and the full freemium structure. Sprints 4, 5, 6 (Hold, Compose, Perform) ship in later releases as primarily premium content. 2. Target audience Primary persona: mid-career professionals aged 25 to 45, with 3 to 10 years of experience in client-facing or stakeholder-management roles. Three specific user types anchor the design: � Sahana, 32, sales account manager. Wants to handle pushback better and close more confidently. Practises before key calls. Will pay for premium if AI feedback feels personalised to her industry. � Daniel, 30, finance professional. Anxious in advisory meetings. Talks too fast under pressure. Wants measurable proof of improvement. Free-tier metrics are his core value. � Priya, 35, transitioning to product management. Interview-prep focused. Needs help structuring answers and projecting authority. Most likely to subscribe during her job-search window. Secondary: early-career professionals (22 to 28) preparing for promotion conversations. Tertiary: founders and self-employed consultants. Geographic launch: English-speaking markets first (UK, India, US, Australia). Indian English warrants explicit acoustic-model testing throughout content production and onboarding QA. Accent variability is the largest free-tier quality risk and a deliberate focus area for the team. 3. The locked 5-step session This is the product spine. Every user, free or premium, completes this exact sequence each session. No shortcuts, no skips. Total duration is approximately five minutes. What evolves across the 36-session arc is the content selected for each step, never the structure itself. Step Duration What it is Why it is required 1. Centre 30 sec Box breathing, affirmation, or pre-pressure preparation Settles the mind before speech. Borrowed from elite-performance practice. 2. Listen 60 sec Audio tidbit on the concept being practised today (with transcript shown alongside) Required theory. Plays as a briefing before practice, not as a standalone lesson. 3. Do 60-90 sec The challenge itself: an open prompt, a constraint challenge, or a comparative session Active practice. The step whose content varies most across sessions. 4. See 90 sec Forensic feedback: metrics shown large, plus a short written critique Awareness of measurable progress. The step where free vs premium differs in depth. 5. Commit 15 sec Voice one sentence: what you will watch for in tomorrow's real conversations Bridges practice to real life. Accumulates into the session 36 capstone artefact. Why the lock matters � Discipline is the brand promise. Mid-career professionals respect a structured ritual. Five minutes per session is the contract. Allowing skips dilutes the contract and triggers streak-saver behaviour that compromises real learning. � Theory is mandatory but invisible. Embedding the tidbit at step 2 means the user hears the relevant concept 60 seconds before applying it. They cannot avoid theory; they also never have to interrupt their day for a separate lesson. � Psychological tools live in the journey. Affirmations and breathing exercises are integrated into step 1 where they serve a function, not curated as a separate section the user can ignore. � Commitment creates the shareable artefact. By session 36 the user has 36 short voice commitments. The capstone replays session 1's commitment alongside session 36's. This is the artefact that drives organic growth. � Beta retention safeguard. Monitor session completion rate closely. Target 90 per cent or above. If completion falls below 85 per cent in beta, investigate the Listen step (transcript clarity, audio pacing, voice quality) before considering any structural relaxation. 4. The six-sprint curriculum The 36-session journey is organised into six sprints of six sessions each. Each sprint has a distinct theme named with a verb: what the user can demonstrably do by sprint's end. Sessions 1 to 5 of each sprint are practice sessions. Session 6 of each sprint is a Review session that consolidates and reveals progress. The six sprints Sprint Theme Sessions What the user learns Access 01 Notice 1-6 Hear your own speech patterns for the first time. Filler counts, pace, pauses, basic awareness. Free 02 Steady 7-12 Build the foundation: pace bands, power pauses, downward inflection, filler reduction under constraint. Free 03 Lead 13-18 Take charge of structure: BLUF, Rule of 3, signposting, brevity under pressure. Free (Hot Seat preview at session 18) 04 Hold 19-24 Stay clear under pressure: calibrated questions, recovery, empathy, hostile Q&A. Premium 05 Compose 25-30 Combine all tools with intention: audience-aware delivery, signposting at scale, multi-constraint synthesis. Premium 06 Perform 31-36 Embody it: storytelling, executive presence, memorable closes, capstone reveal at session 36. Premium Gating and progression � Sprint completion. Sprint N+1 begins after the user completes sprint N's Review session (session 6 of the sprint). Skipping is not possible. � Adaptive remediation. If the user underperforms on a Review session, the next sprint's first practice session loops on the weak concept. Rule-based for free, LLM-enhanced for premium. � Spaced repetition. Sprint 3 onwards incorporates random callbacks from earlier sprints. Sprint 6 includes deliberate echoes of sprints 1 through 5. Prevents skill decay. � Skill decay refresher. If a user has not practised in 14 days, the app serves a refresher session on return before resuming new content. 5. The full 36-session journey The complete content map. Key screens Six screens carry the entire application. Everything else is settings or onboarding. 9.1 Today (home) The user's daily landing screen. Shows today's session as a single committed card. No content discovery, no choice paralysis. The product has already decided what the user needs today. � Top. Wordmark plus subtle streak indicator. � Sprint progress strip. Six dashes representing the six sprints. Completed sprints filled with sage. Current sprint marked ink. Future sprints pale parchment. � Today's card. Sprint number and theme name, today's concept, the five steps numbered with timings, single Begin button. � Bottom nav. Today, Journey, Library, Stats. 9.2 Journey The user's view of the whole arc. A vertical timeline showing all six sprints. Completed sprints expanded with sage progress bars showing each session. Current sprint highlighted in ink with a halo around the active session. Future sprints visible but dimmed. � Header. One sentence: "You're on session 14. Two sprints down, four to go." � Timeline body. Six sprint rows on a single vertical spine, each row containing its sprint number, theme verb, one-line description, and a 6-bar progress strip showing session-level completion. � Tap to expand. Tapping a sprint expands it inline to show its six sessions. Past sessions allow tidbit replay and best-session playback. Future sessions show only the day-theme; specific challenges remain hidden until the user arrives. 9.3 The session player The screen the user sees during the 5-step session. Steps 1, 2, and 4 show structured content. Step 3 shows the challenge UI with mic. Step 5 shows a single voice-recording prompt. � Top. Step counter (01/05), timer, current step label. � Body. Step content. Audio tidbits in step 2 show waveform plus transcript scroll. Challenge in step 3 shows the prompt and mic button. � Footer. Single forward action. No back, no skip during a session. 9.4 The See screen Appears between step 3 and step 5 of each session. Determines whether users return tomorrow. � Big metrics. Three numbers shown large in monospace type. � Replay button. Tap to enter Replay-with-Overlay. � Commentary. Free: 1-sentence templated/flash critique. Premium: 2-3 sentence flash critique with transcript-aware commentary. � Audio tidbit replay link. Optional: re-listen to today's step 2 tidbit. 9.5 The Roast (day-zero diagnostic) The highest-stakes screen in the product. Determines whether users complete onboarding or churn at install. Free version shows deterministic metrics with templated commentary parameterised with user numbers. Premium version adds AI-generated commentary on what was said (not just how). The primary install-day conversion moment. 9.6 Replay-with-Overlay The shareable moment. Users record screen-captures of this and post to social channels. The engine of organic growth. � Waveform timeline. Scrolls as audio plays. � Filler markers. Each filler tagged with a floating label at the exact timestamp. � Pause gaps. Shown as visible white space. � Side-by-side mode. Session 1 vs current. Both waveforms scroll synced. � Share button. Generates a 15-second clip with metrics burned in. 10. Closing principles Four principles to anchor every product decision when the specification falls short: � Build with respect for the user. Mid-career professionals are not students. The product addresses them as adults: direct, never patronising. � The free tier is a complete product, not a trap. A user who never converts still gets a working programme. This earns the word-of-mouth that paid acquisition cannot afford. � Deterministic before AI. Every decision starts with the question: can this be a rule on the transcript? If yes, build the rule. AI is the layer for what rules cannot capture. � The 5-step session is the brand. Discipline is the promise. No skips, no shortcuts, every session. Five minutes.
