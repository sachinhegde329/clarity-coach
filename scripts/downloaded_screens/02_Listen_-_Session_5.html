Before we dive into the mechanics of tone, let's observe the trail.

Most professionals end sentences with a slight rise—they sound like questions even when they're statements.

A downward inflection—letting the pitch drop on the last two words—signals certainty without effort.

Think of it as the 'authoritative anchor.' When you let the tail of the sentence drift upwards, you are subconsciously asking for permission.

In the next exercise, we'll practice grounding these statements.

The Rising Inflection

Creates a feeling of uncertainty or seeking approval. Common in collaborative environments but can undermine authority.

The Power Drop

Signals finality and expertise. By dropping the pitch at the end, the listener perceives the speaker as more grounded.
